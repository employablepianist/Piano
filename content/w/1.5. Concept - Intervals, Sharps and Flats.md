# Concept - Intervals

Any distance between two notes is an *interval*. Every interval has its own name, and sometimes the same interval even has more than one possible name, depending on which *enharmonic* we spell it with.
<br/>

## Semitones, Whole-tones

A *semitone* (or half-tone) is the name given to shortest possible interval on the piano. A semitone is usually composed of a white key and a black key, but E-F and B-C also create semitones, because there is no black key in between them.

A Whole-tone is the distance of two semitones!

> [!Exercise]
> 
> Practice your chromatic scales and your whole-tone scales to get a feel for these fundamental intervals!

We can use semitones to count other intervals also! Any interval at the piano can be given a name, and that interval can be defined as a specific semitone distance. 
<br/>

## Sharps and Flats

When we replace a note with the note a semitone above it, we call that "sharpening" a note. When we replace a note with the note a semitone below it, we call that "flattening" a note.

Notice that sharps and flats retain the *note name* of the note they're replacing. Every black key has at least two possible names at any given time, and the name we give it depends on what white key it's replacing!
