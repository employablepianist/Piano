# Concept - C Major Scale and the Thumb Cross
<br/>

## Full C Major Scale

So far we've looked at the C major pentascale, which contains the first five notes of the C major scale. To complete the full C major scale, we play all white keys from one C to another. 

This is a one octave scale. If we play yet another C further, that is a two-octave scale. Scales can be any number of octaves long, it all depends on where we end the scale and come back.

Like pentascales, full scales can be played ascending, descending, contrary motion between the hands, and with the notes out of order.

## The Thumb Cross

The C major scale is our first introduction to an incredibly important technical element. *The Thumb Cross* occurs when we pass the thumb underneath the palm in order to facilitate a change in position. 

In this case, the thumb cross occurs in the right hand in between the notes E and F. In the left hand it occurs in between the notes G and A.

When crossing the thumb, don't curl the fingers. Instead, let your arm move you over while retaining a neutral curvature of the hand.

# Alternate Fingerings

It is *great* to have a standard fingering we play any scale or mode with, however it is also quite useful to experiment with putting the thumb cross in different parts of the scale! We needn't always play scales with 1 2 3 1 2 3 4, and often in repertoire we'll be forced to find alternate fingerings for scales. Start experimenting right away with alternate fingerings! Feel free to get creative.
