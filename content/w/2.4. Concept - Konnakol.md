# Concept - Konnakol

Konnakol is a system of syllables used to vocalize rhythms. It can be used at the piano or away from the piano, it's an instrument agnostic approach to rhythm.

Konnakol is great for pianists because we can use it to sing rhythms aloud while we play pieces. It can also be great for us to take note of the fact that oftentimes rhythms will repeat even when pitch changes!

In Konnakol, we give groupings syllables based on their size. Here are examples from 1 through 9:

- 1 - Ta
- 2 - Ta Ki
- 3 - Ta Ki Ta
- 4 - Ta Ka Di Mi
- 5 - Ta Di Gi Na Tum
- 6 - Ta Ka Di Mi Ta Ka
- 7 - Ta Ka Di Mi Ta Ki Ta
- 8 - Ta Ka Di Mi Ta Ka Jo Nu
- 9 - Ta Ka Di Mi Ta Di Gi Na Tum


> [!Exercises]
> 1. Sing through some of your material on konnakol. 
> 2. Create your own rhythms, perhaps to start the composition of a piece.

## Connecting Konnakol to Western Notation

A great way to learn western notational rhythms is to write them out as Konnakol! 

One thing I love to do is to write a Konnakol rhythm out and underline the rhythmic attacks. Then I like to speak the Konnakol out loud while I clap the underlines syllables.

Try it here on half-notes:
<br/>

<u>Ta</u> ka <u>di</u> mi || <u>Ta</u> ka <u>di</u> mi
