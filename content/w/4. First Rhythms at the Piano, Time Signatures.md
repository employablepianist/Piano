## The Beat

Music is divided into “beats”. A “beat” is a single instance of the most basic pulse of music. Any time you’ve counted a long to a song, or tapped your foot along to a song, you’ve been tapping to “the beat”. Rhythmic notation tells you:

1. On which beat a note is played.
    
2. How many beats the note sustains for.
    

The first three beats learned in this book are whole notes, half notes, and quarter notes.

## Whole note

Receives four beats of sound. The equivalent silence is the whole rest, which receives four beats of silence.

## Half note

Receives two beats of sound. The equivalent silence is the half rest, which receives two beats of silence.

## Quarter note

Receives one beat of sound. The equivalent silence is the quarter rest, which receives one beat of silence.

## Time Signatures

Notice the long vertical lines crossing through the staff. These are *bar lines*, used to differentiate between *measures*. 

*Measures* are used in music to divide things up into rhythmic chunks. This helps us organize the music in our heads so we can play it more easily, but it also gives us a clue regarding how the music is supposed to sound.

Notice in Mikrokosmos, our first piece, that there is a large "4/4" marking sitting at the front of the staff. We sort of read time signatures like fractions, if we were to read fractions very literally. This means we would read "4/4" as "Four Quarters".

The top number of the time signature tells us *how many beats will be in each measure*. In other words, in 4/4, every time you see a measure you can feel sure that there will be four beats in that measure.

The bottom number of the time signature tells us *what type of note constitutes a beat*. In 4/4, the quarter note is the beat, so each measure has four quarter notes (or equivalent). 

> More examples
> 
> In 3/4, the quarter note is the beat and we have 3 beats per measure. Therefore we have 3 quarter notes per measure.
> 
> In 2/4, the quarter note is the beat and we have 2 beats per measure. Therefore we have 2 quarter notes per measure.
> 
> In 3/8, the eighth note is the beat and we have 3 beats per measure. Therefore we have 3 eighth notes per measure.
> 
> In 5/8, the eighth note is the beat and we have 5 beats per measure. Therefore we have 5 eighth notes per measure.
