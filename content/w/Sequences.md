A "Sequence" happens when we move a pattern through a key or chord progression.

Here are two very useful sequences to start off with, we'll call them sequence A and B:

## Sequence A

Sequence A involves taking a three-note cell and moving it up and down. Notice that the bottom note of every three-note cell is moving up by one step each time. You can play with this distance to create more variations of this sequence.

![Sequence 1](/Piano/images/Sequences-1.svg)

## Sequence B

Sequence B behaves the same way, this time with a 4-note cell.

![Sequence 2](/Piano/images/Sequences-2.svg)

## Moving Sequences Through Keys

Notice here I'll write sequence A again, this time adding one black key, F-sharp. In the event that we add a black key, we replace the white key that shares its name! For example, in the key of G we play F-sharp *instead* of F, not in addition to.

![Sequence 3](/Piano/images/Sequences-3_copy.svg)

Same here on sequence B, this time with B-flat *replacing* B-natural.

![Sequence 4](/Piano/images/Sequences-4.svg)

## Variations on Sequences A and B

To create digestible variations for sequences A and B, start by playing each cell descending instead of ascending. You may also wish to move the sequence downwards instead of upwards.
