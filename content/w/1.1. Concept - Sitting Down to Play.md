# Concept - Sitting down to Play
<br/>

## Seating and Posture

For best results, sit far enough away from the piano that your elbows can just barely pass by your stomach.

When sitting, it’s good to lean just slightly forward, so that your torso feels balanced slightly towards the keys. Remember that it’s easier to move back to a neutral position than it is to move back from a neutral position.

Sit at such a height that your elbow is level with the white keytops.
<br/>

## Hand Posture

Drop your hand to your side and look at it in a mirror. This is your hand and wrist posture for piano playing! Everyone’s hand looks different, but most hands do not naturally take up a perfect five note position - this is ok!

### Some things you’ll notice:

1. If your thumb is touching a white key, your other fingers will be resting on, in, or around the black keys (in the black-key-area).
    
2. If your longer fingers (index, middle) are in the white-key-area, the thumb will be off the keys.
    
3. Your wrist will remain neutral, not dipping - and you may feel at the moment you don’t have the power to play keys. We’ll get to this! The solution is not to curl the fingers, but rather to coordinate the forearm to help you put keys down.

## Leg Positioning

Try at first to have both feet flat on the floor in such a way that your weight is balanced between your sit-bones on the bench, and your two feet. Oftentimes pianists will also tuck their left leg slightly behind their center of gravity in order to achieve lumbar support.

Having the feet flat on the floor can be incredibly helpful when it comes to spanning the vertical distances required to play the piano. Oftentimes we can feel as if we’re “pushing off” one of our feet in order to move over.

Keep in mind that one or both of the feet will often be used to operate the piano’s pedals.

## First Movement at the Piano

The most fundamental movement of piano playing is the up-down movement of the forearm. To achieve this feeling, bend your arm at the elbow, moving your hand, wrist, and forearm slightly up towards your ear. Now, bring this whole unit down in order to depress the key. From now on this motion will be the basic “down” motion. Later on we’ll discuss more movements.
